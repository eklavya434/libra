## 1. WHAT: What Was Built

Phase 47 implements **Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO)** and **Online On-Policy Direct Preference Optimization (Online DPO)** for educational language models in Project Libra from mathematical first principles in PyTorch. It bridges the gap between static pairwise preference rankings (Phase 22 DPO) and real-world alignment feedback signals.

Key Deliverables:
1. **Unpaired Binary Alignment (`packages/training/kto_dataset.py` & `packages/training/kto_trainer.py`)**:
   - `KTOSample` & `KTODataset`: Manages unpaired binary feedback $(x, y, z)$ where $z \in \{+1, -1\}$ indicates desirable vs undesirable completions, with prompt masking (-100) for completion-only evaluation.
   - `KTOTrainer`: Computes implicit rewards $r_\theta(x, y) = \beta (\log \pi_\theta(y \mid x) - \log \pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x))$, establishes the dynamic reference point $z_{\text{ref}} = \mathbb{E}[r_\theta(x, y)]$, and optimizes via the asymmetric Kahneman-Tversky prospect loss.
2. **On-Policy Exploration Engine (`packages/training/online_dpo_trainer.py`)**:
   - `OnlineDPOTrainer`: Samples candidate completions on-policy at current policy temperature, evaluates them dynamically with a reward oracle / scoring function, constructs winning ($y_w$) and losing ($y_l$) pairs, and applies DPO updates, eliminating off-policy distribution shift.
3. **Multi-Paradigm Alignment Evaluator (`packages/evaluation/alignment_eval.py`)**:
   - `AlignmentEvaluator`: Benchmarks SFT / Reference, Offline DPO, Online DPO, and KTO across win rates against reference, implicit reward separation, policy KL drift, and response length.
4. **FastAPI Endpoints (`apps/backend/api/v1/endpoints/kto.py`)**:
   - `POST /api/v1/kto/step`: Executes a KTO training step on binary feedback samples.
   - `POST /api/v1/kto/online-dpo/step`: Executes on-policy rollouts and online DPO optimization.
   - `POST /api/v1/kto/evaluate`: Runs comparative evaluation of alignment paradigms.
   - `GET /api/v1/kto/presets`: Supplies educational presets (helpfulness, refusal safety, prospect theory parameters).
5. **Interactive Next.js Alignment Lab UI (`apps/frontend/src/components/KTOView.tsx`)**:
   - Interactive Kahneman-Tversky S-shaped value function visualizer with adjustable loss aversion multiplier ($\lambda_U / \lambda_D$).
   - Binary feedback collector with 1-click 👍 Desirable / 👎 Undesirable toggles.
   - On-policy rollout arena displaying generated candidates, oracle rewards, and DPO margin.
   - Comparative paradigm arena table comparing SFT, Offline DPO, Online DPO, and KTO.

## 2. WHY: Why It Exists & What Problem It Solves

### The Pairwise Bottleneck in Standard DPO
In standard DPO (Phase 22) and RLHF, training data consists of paired preferences $(x, y_{\text{chosen}}, y_{\text{rejected}})$. However:
1. **Unpaired Reality**: Real user feedback is almost exclusively binary and unpaired: thumbs up/down, accepted/rejected, upvoted/downvoted.
2. **Annotation Cost**: Generating two distinct responses per prompt and paying annotators to rank them costs more than double the expense of single binary labels.
3. **Confounding Pairs**: Synthetic pairs can introduce length bias, stylistic artifacts, or artificial contrasts.

### Behavioral Economics & Prospect Theory
KTO grounds model alignment in Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky's Nobel Prize-winning **Prospect Theory**:
- Humans evaluate outcomes not in absolute terms, but relative to a subjective **reference point** ($z_{\text{ref}}$).
- **Loss Aversion**: The psychological pain of a loss is roughly $1.5\times$ to $2\times$ greater than the pleasure of an equivalent gain ($\lambda_U > \lambda_D$).
- By embedding this asymmetry directly into the loss function, models are penalized significantly more for generating harmful or undesirable text than they are rewarded for generating safe text.

### The Off-Policy Distribution Shift Problem
In offline DPO, preference pairs are sampled from a static dataset collected from an earlier base policy. As the policy $\pi_\theta$ updates during training, the probability distribution of tokens generated by $\pi_\theta$ diverges from the offline dataset. **Online DPO** solves this by sampling candidates directly from $\pi_\theta$ at each training step and scoring them in the loop.

## 3. HOW: Mathematical Foundations & Code Implementation

### A. Implicit Reward Formulation
For prompt $x$ and completion $y$:
$$r_\theta(x, y) = \beta \left( \log \pi_\theta(y \mid x) - \log \pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x) \right)$$
where $\beta$ controls the strength of the KL divergence penalty against the frozen reference policy $\pi_{\text{ref}}$.

### B. The Reference Point $z_{\text{ref}}$
In KTO, the reference point represents the expected reward under the policy:
$$z_{\text{ref}} = \mathbb{E}_{(x, y)} [r_\theta(x, y)]$$
Detaching $z_{\text{ref}}$ from the autograd graph ensures numerical stability and provides a centered baseline for gains vs losses.

### C. The Kahneman-Tversky Asymmetric Loss Function
Let $v(u) = 1 - \sigma(u)$ (or $-\log \sigma(u)$).
- **For Desirable Completions ($y \in \mathcal{Y}_D$, $z = +1$)**:
  $$\mathcal{L}_D = \lambda_D \cdot \left( 1 - \sigma(r_\theta(x, y) - z_{\text{ref}}) \right)$$
- **For Undesirable Completions ($y \in \mathcal{Y}_U$, $z = -1$)**:
  $$\mathcal{L}_U = \lambda_U \cdot \left( 1 - \sigma(z_{\text{ref}} - r_\theta(x, y)) \right)$$
- **Combined KTO Objective**:
  $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{KTO}} = \frac{1}{|D|} \sum_{i \in D} \mathcal{L}_D(x_i, y_i) + \frac{1}{|U|} \sum_{j \in U} \mathcal{L}_U(x_j, y_j)$$
With $\lambda_U = 1.33$ and $\lambda_D = 1.0$, the loss gradient for an undesirable output is 33% stronger than for a desirable output, actively discouraging dangerous failure modes.

### D. Online DPO On-Policy Exploration Loop
At each training step:
1. Sample prompts $x \sim \mathcal{D}_{\text{prompts}}$.
2. Generate $K \ge 2$ candidates $[y_1, \dots, y_K] \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid x)$ at temperature $\tau$.
3. Score candidates with reward oracle: $s_k = R(x, y_k)$.
4. Assign winner $y_w = \text{argmax}_k(s_k)$ and loser $y_l = \text{argmin}_k(s_k)$.
5. Apply standard DPO loss step:
   $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}} = -\log \sigma \left( \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w \mid x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l \mid x)} \right)$$
