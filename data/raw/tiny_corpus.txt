Libra is an educational laboratory and artificial intelligence assistant.
Large language models learn patterns from text by predicting the next token.
First, text is converted into numbers called tokens.
Next, tokens are mapped into vectors called embeddings.
Attention allows tokens to look at other tokens in the context.
Causal masking ensures that tokens can only look into the past, never into the future.
The multi-head attention mechanism combines information from different representation subspaces.
A feed-forward neural network processes each token representation independently.
Residual connections and layer normalization help gradients flow smoothly during backpropagation.
The model outputs numbers called logits for every word in the vocabulary.
Cross entropy loss measures how well the model predicted the next token.
An optimizer like AdamW updates the parameters to decrease the loss over time.
With each training step, the loss decreases, and the predictions become more accurate.
Libra is designed to run efficiently on a standard CPU with zero cost.
Knowledge and understanding grow through experimentation and first principles.
